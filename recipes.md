# Recipes

Outcome: three habits from the lab's
garden notebook, each assembled only
from machinery earlier chapters
verified — a season log you never
have to file, an inventory that stays
honest, and a weekly review that
takes ten minutes.

## 1. A season log that files itself

**You need:** daily notes (chapter 9)
and linked mentions (chapter 5).

**Method.** Write the day in the day.
`Planted out the [[Tomatoes]]` in
April's second daily note is the
whole technique. When October asks
*how did the tomatoes' season go*,
open `Tomatoes.md` and read its
backlinks (chapter 6): every dated
mention, in context, in order — a
season log assembled from entries
you wrote for their own sake. The
lab's tomato note already shows the
shape: six backlinks, the first of
them the planting-out day.

**The rule it teaches.** You do not
maintain logs of things. You mention
things, in the day's note, with
brackets on — and the log maintains
itself.

## 2. An inventory that stays honest

**You need:** properties (chapter 7),
one template (chapter 9), and a front
door (chapter 4).

**Method.** The lab's `Seed
Inventory.md` carries `type:
inventory` and an `updated` date in
its front matter, and one table in
its prose. The recipe is the
placement of the update habit: the
front door links to the inventory,
the inventory's `updated` property
is changed on the day the tin is
opened, and nothing enters the table
without the tin being open in front
of you. Inventories rot in every
tool ever made; the only known
preservative is a short walk between
the record and the thing, taken at
the moment of change.

**The rule it teaches.** A record is
honest exactly as long as updating
it is easier than believing it.

## 3. The ten-minute weekly review

**You need:** the week's daily notes,
the front door, and a `Weekly
Review` note made fresh each week.

**Method.** On the week's last day,
open the seven daily notes and the
review note side by side. In the
review, write three short lists:
*done*, *carried*, *let go* — linking
each item to the day it happened
with a wikilink. Anything in
*carried* for the second week running
gets a note of its own, named for
the thing, per chapter 4 — it has
graduated from the diary into the
notebook, and it will collect its
own backlinks from there. Delete
nothing; the dailies are the record,
the review is the reading of it.

**The rule it teaches.** A notebook
is reviewed forward, not organised
backward. The review is where the
week becomes knowledge; the filing
was done by the links, on the days
themselves.

Check: every step of all three
recipes names its chapter, and every
chapter named was verified in the
lab — links counted, properties
parsed, the app itself opened on the
same files. A recipe book whose
ingredients were each weighed in
front of you is this guide's idea of
a fair ending to the how-to half of
the book.
